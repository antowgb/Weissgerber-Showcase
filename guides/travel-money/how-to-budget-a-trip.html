How to budget a trip

Short answer: break the trip into categories, estimate a daily cost for each based on real research about your destination, add a buffer, then track spending as you go instead of guessing at the end. Here's how to do each step.

By Antonin Weissgerber · Last updated August 6, 2026

1. Break the trip into categories

A single lump-sum guess is almost always wrong, because it hides where the money actually goes. Split your trip into a handful of categories before you estimate anything: accommodation, food, local transport, activities and entrance fees, and a miscellaneous bucket for the things you forgot to plan for.

2. Estimate a daily cost per category

For each category, research a realistic daily cost specific to your destination and the way you like to travel. Accommodation prices for a hostel dorm, a mid-range hotel, and a private rental can differ by several multiples in the same city, so use sources for the exact type of trip you're planning rather than a generic country-wide average. Do the same for food (street food and self-catering versus restaurants) and transport (public transport versus taxis or rented cars).

Multiply each daily estimate by the number of days you'll actually be in that mode, since a trip that mixes cities and slower stops rarely has a flat daily cost throughout.

3. Add fixed costs separately

Flights, visas, travel insurance, and any big one-off purchases (gear, a rail pass) don't scale with the number of days, so estimate them separately and add them on top of your daily-cost total rather than folding them into a daily average.

4. Build in a buffer

Prices move, plans change, and something unplanned almost always comes up. Rather than picking a fixed percentage, size the buffer to how uncertain your plans still are: a trip with fixed dates and most things pre-booked needs less padding than a loosely planned itinerary or one crossing several countries with different cost levels. A rule of thumb I use myself is to round each category estimate up rather than down, then let that rounding be the buffer, so it stays intentional spending room instead of an arbitrary add-on.

5. Track spending as you go

A budget only helps if you compare it against actual spending while the trip is happening, not after you're home. Log expenses as they happen, in whatever currency they occurred, and keep a running total converted back into a single reference currency. That's the only way to know on day 4 of a 10-day trip whether you're on pace or already behind.

6. Review and adjust mid-trip

Check your remaining budget against your remaining days periodically, not just at the very end. If a category is running hot, it's usually easier to pull back on discretionary spending like dining out or optional activities than on costs you've already committed to, like a booked hotel.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I budget per day for a trip?

There's no universal number, it depends heavily on the destination and your travel style. A more reliable approach is to estimate a daily cost per category (accommodation, food, transport, activities) based on research specific to where you're going, rather than relying on a generic daily figure.

How big should my travel budget buffer be?

There's no universal percentage that fits every trip. Size the buffer to how uncertain your itinerary still is: fully booked, fixed-date trips need less padding, while loosely planned or multi-country trips need more. A simple personal rule is to round each category estimate up rather than down and treat that gap as the buffer.

Should I budget in my home currency or the local currency?

Set your overall budget in your home currency so it stays a single, consistent number, but expect to log individual expenses in the local currency as they happen and convert them automatically.

What if I go over budget mid-trip?

Check your remaining days against your remaining budget and adjust categories rather than panicking. It's usually easier to trim discretionary spending like activities or dining out than fixed costs like accommodation already booked.
