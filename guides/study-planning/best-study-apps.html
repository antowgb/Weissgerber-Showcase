Best study apps for students in 2026

Most "best study app" lists are written by people who haven't used any of them. Worse, they mix up two different problems: not knowing what to study, and not managing to study once you've decided. Here's an honest comparison by which problem each app actually solves.

Vitae

Approach: a study planner with focus sessions built in. You enter your subjects, exams and free hours; it schedules your week, then recommends the next session and shows why it picked that one. Sessions run with a Pomodoro timer, ambient sound, and binaural audio if you want it.

Price: free to download, one-time purchase for Premium, no subscription. Planner, exam mode and Pomodoro are free.

Best for: students with exams on the calendar who lose time deciding what to revise, and want the planning and the session in the same app.

Works offline. Headphones only matter for the optional binaural layer.

Brain.fm

Approach: AI-generated functional music designed to guide focus, relaxation, or sleep. Not binaural beats, a different audio method, backed by in-house research.

Price: subscription-based (check brain.fm for current pricing, it has changed more than once this year).

Best for: people who already know what to work on and want better audio for it. No planning, no exam tracking, not student-specific.

Forest

Approach: locks your phone and grows a virtual tree while you work, no audio, pure anti-distraction mechanic.

Price: one-time purchase on iOS, with some subscription features.

Best for: people whose main problem is picking up their phone mid-session, not staying in a focus state once they've started.

Generic Pomodoro timers

Approach: work and break intervals, nothing else. No subjects, no schedule, no record of what you covered.

Price: mostly free.

Best for: people who already know what they're studying and just need the clock. A timer counts your minutes; it doesn't tell you where to spend them.

Notion, Todoist and general planners

Approach: flexible task and note systems you shape into a study system yourself.

Price: free tiers, paid plans for the rest.

Best for: people who enjoy building the system. The trade-off is that the setup is on you, it doesn't know your exam dates unless you maintain them, and nothing runs the session at the end.

Spotify / lo-fi playlists

Approach: background music, huge free catalog.

Price: free (with ads) or subscription.

Best for: people who just want something playing in the background. No structure, no session tracking, no focus mechanism beyond familiarity.

How to choose

Start with which of the two problems is actually yours.

  • You don't know what to study next, and have exams to spread out: Vitae
  • You want to design your own study system from scratch: Notion or Todoist
  • You know what to study and want audio that helps you stay in it: Brain.fm
  • Your main issue is picking up your phone, not concentration itself: Forest
  • You already have a routine and just need a clock: any free Pomodoro app
  • You just want something playing: Spotify

None of these fix a lack of sleep or an unmanageable workload. And a tool that only makes focusing easier still leaves you the decision that costs the most time: which subject, for how long, and whether it's the one the exam calendar actually demands.
