Vitae vs Brain.fm: study planner or focus audio?

They get compared because both end up playing in your headphones while you work. But one is a study planner that runs the session, and the other is an audio catalog. Here's the actual comparison, not a sales pitch for either.

The core difference

These two apps are answers to different questions.

Brain.fm answers "what should I listen to while I work?". It is an audio service: a catalog of AI-generated functional music with embedded rhythmic patterns, engineered to hold attention without lyrics or hooks. You choose a mental state, press play, and work.

Vitae answers "what should I study, and when?". It is a study planner that happens to run the session too. You enter your subjects, your exams and the hours you're free; it builds a study week, recommends the next session with a reason attached, then plays it with a Pomodoro timer and, if you want, ambient sound and binaural audio underneath.

So the comparison is not really binaural beats versus functional music. It is a planner with audio built in versus an audio catalog with no planner.

Feature by feature

Study planning

Vitae: a weekly planner built from your availability, with editable study blocks, a calendar view and the option to regenerate the week. Free in the app.

Brain.fm: none. It plays audio; scheduling your week is your problem.

Exam tracking

Vitae: exams with a date and a target number of hours, showing days left, hours done, hours remaining and the daily target. Exams within ten days take priority in what the app recommends next.

Brain.fm: none.

Focus sessions

Vitae: multi-step guided sessions (study flow, exam focus, sprint, recovery) with a Pomodoro mode that alternates focus blocks and breaks. Three sessions free, the rest with Premium.

Brain.fm: timed listening sessions by mental state, with a timer, but no session structure tied to what you are studying.

Audio approach

Vitae: binaural tones plus ambient layers, as a supporting layer around the session. Binaural needs headphones; everything else works on a speaker.

Brain.fm: AI-generated functional music as the product itself, no headphones required, backed by their own in-house research.

Offline

Vitae: fully offline. Audio ships with the app, your plan and statistics stay on your device, and reminders are local notifications.

Brain.fm: streams its catalog, with offline downloads on paid plans. Check their current terms, since that can change independently of this page.

Pricing

Vitae: free to download, with a one-time lifetime Premium unlock. No subscription, no ads.

Brain.fm: subscription only. Pricing has changed more than once in 2026, so check brain.fm directly for the current rate.

Target audience

Vitae: students, specifically. The whole model assumes subjects, exams and a term.

Brain.fm: a general audience of professionals, creatives and students, covering focus, relaxation and sleep.

What's claimed

Neither app is a clinically proven treatment for any condition, and both say so. Vitae's recommendation engine is a fixed set of rules over data you entered, not a model and not a prediction, and it shows you which rule it used.

When Brain.fm is the better fit

  • You already know what to work on and just want better audio for it
  • You don't want to wear headphones
  • You want one tool covering focus, relaxation, and sleep, not just studying
  • You're fine with a subscription for continuous access to new content

When Vitae is the better fit

  • The hard part is deciding what to study, not what to listen to
  • You have exams with dates and need your revision spread across the weeks left
  • You want the planning, the exam tracking and the timer in the same place as the session
  • You want a one-time purchase instead of a recurring cost

They are not mutually exclusive, either. Nothing stops you from planning your week in Vitae and playing Brain.fm underneath, if that's the audio you prefer.
