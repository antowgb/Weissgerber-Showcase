What a binaural beat actually is

Each ear receives a slightly different frequency through your headphones. Your brain can't reconcile the two directly, so it constructs a third, perceived tone from the gap between them, and your brainwaves tend to follow that new rhythm. It isn't a metaphor; it's how the auditory system processes phase difference between two ears.

How a session works

1

Two tones, two ears

One frequency plays in the left ear, a slightly different one in the right: for example 100 Hz and 108 Hz.

2

The brain builds a third

The 8 Hz gap between them is perceived as a single pulsing beat, distinct from either original tone.

3

Brainwaves follow along

Sustained exposure nudges your brainwaves toward that rhythm, into a state suited to studying or relaxing.

4

You start the session

Pick Alpha for calm focus or Theta for deep study, add an ambient layer, set the timer, and begin.

What's inside

Alpha waves, 8-14 Hz

Calm, sustained concentration for reading, writing, or working through a problem without fatigue.

Theta waves, 4-8 Hz

Deeper study and memorization, suited to intensive review and exam preparation.
