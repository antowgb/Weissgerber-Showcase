Vitae vs Brain.fm: which focus app fits students?

Vitae and Brain.fm both use audio to help you focus, but they're built on different methods and for different audiences. Here's the actual comparison, not a sales pitch for either.

The core difference

Vitae uses binaural beats: two slightly different tones, one per ear, that your brain resolves into a third perceived rhythm. It requires headphones.

Brain.fm uses AI-generated functional music with embedded rhythmic patterns, engineered to hold attention without lyrics or hooks. It doesn't require headphones.

Both are audio-based focus tools. Neither is a clinically proven treatment for any condition, both make that clear in their own research disclaimers.

Pricing

Vitae

One-time purchase, lifetime access, no subscription.

Brain.fm

Subscription only (pricing has changed multiple times in 2026, check brain.fm directly for the current rate).

If you don't want a recurring cost, Vitae's model fits better by design. If you want continuous access to a large, evolving catalog of AI-generated tracks, a subscription tool like Brain.fm makes more sense.

Who each one is built for

Vitae is built around study sessions specifically: exam prep, revision, guided sessions with a Pomodoro timer layered in. It's aimed at students.

Brain.fm is broader: focus, relaxation, meditation, and sleep, aimed at a general audience of professionals, creatives, and students alike. It's not student-specific.

Offline use

Vitae generates all audio on-device and works fully offline once installed.

Brain.fm streams AI-generated tracks and offers offline downloads on paid plans, but check their current offering, since app features change independently of this page.

When Brain.fm is the better fit

  • You don't want to wear headphones
  • You want one tool covering focus, relaxation, and sleep
  • You're fine with a subscription for continuous access to new content

When Vitae is the better fit

  • You want a one-time purchase, not a recurring cost
  • You're specifically building a study routine, not a general focus tool
  • You're fine wearing headphones for binaural separation to work
